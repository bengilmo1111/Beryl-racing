# Eastbourne Dash — Track Product Requirements Document

**Game:** Beryl Racing  
**Course ID:** `eastbourne-dash`  
**Format:** Point-to-point time trial  
**Target duration:** Approximately two minutes  
**Setting:** Days Bay and Eastbourne, Lower Hutt  
**Time:** Warm late afternoon

Read with:

- `PRD.md`
- `docs/ART-DIRECTION.md`
- `docs/ART-PRODUCTION.md`
- `docs/tracks/EASTBOURNE-ART-BRIEF.md`

---

## 1. Course fantasy

**Eastbourne Dash** is a relaxed but competitive coastal time trial from 28 Ferry Road in Days Bay to the Eastbourne RSA.

The player joins Marine Drive and follows one recognisable ribbon of coastal road south past Days Bay, Williams Park and Rona Bay, then reaches the Eastbourne village area, turns inland and completes a short technical final run to the RSA.

The course should feel scenic, flowing and familiar. Most of the drive rewards carrying momentum through long waterfront bends. The final inland section changes the rhythm with tighter streets and sharper turns before the finish.

“Dash” describes the tone, not the scoring. Players are still trying to set the fastest time, but the course should never feel like a formal motorsport circuit.

---

## 2. Geographic basis

The course is based on the real driving route from:

- **Start:** 28 Ferry Road, Days Bay.
- **Finish:** Eastbourne RSA, 48 Tuatoru Street, Eastbourne.

The route should broadly follow:

1. 28 Ferry Road.
2. Join Marine Drive.
3. Days Bay and Williams Park.
4. Long coastal run south.
5. Rona Bay waterfront.
6. Eastbourne village approach.
7. Inland turn around the Muritai Road / Oroua Street area.
8. Short technical residential section.
9. Eastbourne RSA finish near Tuatoru Street.

The course must preserve the broad direction and sequence of the real route. It may compress distance, simplify intersections and combine minor road details to fit an approximately two-minute run.

## 3. Course rhythm

### Section 1 — Ferry Road start

Purpose:

- Introduce the controls without immediate punishment.
- Establish the harbour-and-hills setting.
- Move the player quickly towards Marine Drive.

Driving:

- Short opening acceleration zone.
- One gentle setup turn.
- Clear and immediate route guidance.
- No confusing residential side roads.

### Section 2 — Days Bay and Williams Park

Required recognition cues:

- Days Bay waterfront.
- Days Bay Wharf visible from the road.
- Williams Park character.
- Beach, harbour and bush-covered hills.

Driving:

- Broad, flowing coastal curves.
- Strong sightlines.
- Enough width for inexperienced players.
- Continuous protection from water and beach access.

### Section 3 — Marine Drive coastal run

This is the main high-speed body of the course.

Driving:

- Long waterfront bends.
- Sustained momentum.
- Gentle changes in radius.
- Harbour on the coastal side and bush or settlement on the inland side.
- Barriers or walls where the real road is exposed to water.

The road should read as one continuous coastal ribbon rather than a sequence of disconnected landmark arenas.

### Section 4 — Rona Bay stretch

Required recognition cues:

- Rona Bay waterfront character.
- A gradual increase in settlement density.
- Transition from open coastal road towards the Eastbourne village area.

Driving:

- Slightly tighter curves.
- Reduced sightline length.
- Preparation for the more technical final section.

### Section 5 — Eastbourne village approach

Required recognition cues:

- Eastbourne shops or village character.
- Muritai School area may be used as a supporting local marker.
- More enclosed road edges and low-rise buildings.

Driving:

- Lower ideal speed.
- Tighter bends or turns.
- Strong directional arrows.
- Side roads simplified, blocked or visually de-emphasised.

### Section 6 — Inland turn and RSA finish

The route leaves the waterfront and turns inland around the Muritai Road / Oroua Street area before reaching Tuatoru Street and the RSA.

Driving:

- One obvious inland turn.
- One or two sharper residential corners.
- Short technical final run.
- Finish line clearly associated with the Eastbourne RSA.

The final section should feel like a small sting in the tail after the long coastal flow.

---

## 4. Driving identity

Primary driving features:

- Long waterfront bends.
- Sustained coastal momentum.
- A gradual tightening through Rona Bay and the village.
- A short technical inland finish.

The course rewards:

- Smooth steering.
- Avoiding unnecessary braking on Marine Drive.
- Slowing before the village and inland turn.
- Clean acceleration out of the final corners.

It should not require:

- Advanced drifting.
- Handbrake turns.
- Hidden shortcuts.
- Precise simulator-style throttle control.

---

## 5. Route guidance

The course must be obvious to a seven-year-old and a 75-year-old.

Use:

- A single visually dominant route.
- Strong road-edge treatment.
- Large advance arrows before the inland turn and final sharp corners.
- Barriers, buildings and vegetation to close false routes.
- A clearly visible RSA finish marker.

Do not use:

- Open side streets that appear equally valid.
- Decorative beach access that looks driveable.
- Dead ends.
- A minimap as the primary navigation method.

---

## 6. Barriers and off-road behaviour

- The player cannot drive onto Days Bay Beach, Rona Bay beach areas or into the harbour.
- Coastal barriers, walls, vegetation or road geometry physically prevent access.
- Barrier collisions bounce Beryl back and reduce speed.
- Inland grass follows the shared slow-and-spinny rule.
- Buildings, walls and major roadside objects are solid where collision is plausible.
- Track geometry must avoid places where Beryl can become permanently stuck.

---

## 7. Timing and checkpoints

- The timer begins at the start signal or start gate, consistently with the shared game system.
- Ordered checkpoints follow the real route progression.
- Checkpoints prevent bypassing Marine Drive, the village approach or the inland final section.
- Checkpoints should remain invisible during normal play unless needed for debugging.
- The route should take approximately two minutes for a competent player.

---

## 8. Presentation and audio

### Lighting

- Warm late-afternoon sun.
- Excellent gameplay visibility.
- Soft long shadows.
- Golden highlights on Beryl’s chrome.
- Harbour water with restrained warm reflections.

### Music

- Breezy, 1960s-inspired coastal driving music.
- Surf influence without parody.
- Lighter and more relaxed than the Remutaka track.

### Beryl

- Engine rises naturally on the long coastal acceleration sections.
- Tyres screech during tighter village and RSA turns.
- Body lean is readable through the waterfront bends.
- Barrier impacts sound comic and non-destructive.

---

## 9. Finish presentation

Finish location:

- Eastbourne RSA, near Tuatoru Street.

Finish message:

> **Phew! Just in time for a beer.**

The finish should feel like arriving at a familiar community destination, not winning a professional race.

The results screen shows:

- Final time.
- Top-three status.
- Arcade-style name entry if qualified.
- Retry.
- Change course.

---

## 10. Out of scope for the first release

- Moving traffic.
- Moving ferry.
- Pedestrians.
- Beachgoers or swimmers.
- Driveable beach access.
- Exact recreation of every house or shop.
- Dense modern signage.
- Alternative routes.
- Random hazards.

---

## 11. Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The course begins at 28 Ferry Road.
- [ ] The player joins Marine Drive and follows a recognisable coastal route south.
- [ ] Days Bay Wharf, Williams Park, Rona Bay and the Eastbourne village area are recognisable in broad strokes.
- [ ] The route turns inland near the Muritai Road / Oroua Street area.
- [ ] The course finishes at Eastbourne RSA near Tuatoru Street.
- [ ] The course reads as one continuous ribbon road rather than disconnected scenes.
- [ ] Long waterfront bends provide the main high-speed rhythm.
- [ ] The final inland section provides tighter technical turns.
- [ ] Beaches and harbour are visible but unreachable.
- [ ] No side road creates a confusing route choice or dead end.
- [ ] The course takes approximately two minutes for a competent player.
- [ ] Lighting reads as warm late afternoon.
- [ ] Music has a distinct breezy 1960s coastal identity.
- [ ] The finish message reads: “Phew! Just in time for a beer.”
- [ ] The local Eastbourne top three stores times and names correctly.
