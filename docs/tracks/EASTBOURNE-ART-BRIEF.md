# Eastbourne Pootle — Track Art Brief

**Course:** Eastbourne Pootle  
**Route:** 28 Ferry Road → Marine Drive → Days Bay / Williams Park → Rona Bay → Eastbourne village → inland turn near Muritai Road / Oroua Street → Eastbourne RSA  
**Time:** Warm late afternoon  
**Mood:** Breezy, familiar, relaxed and gently celebratory

Read with:

- `docs/ART-DIRECTION.md`
- `docs/ART-PRODUCTION.md`
- `docs/tracks/EASTBOURNE-POOTLE-PRD.md`

---

## 1. Recognition goal

A player who knows Eastbourne and Days Bay should recognise the course from its broad coastal geography, route sequence and atmosphere without expecting an exact aerial map.

The strongest recognition test is:

> Does this feel like driving from Ferry Road through Days Bay and Rona Bay, then turning inland through Eastbourne to reach the RSA?

Recognition depends most on:

- One continuous coastal ribbon road.
- Harbour on the coastal side.
- Bush-covered hills and settlement on the inland side.
- Days Bay Wharf and Williams Park.
- Rona Bay waterfront character.
- Eastbourne village and shops.
- The clear inland turn towards the RSA.
- The RSA as a familiar community destination.

Exact building dimensions and GPS-level road geometry are not required.

---

## 2. Visual story

The course should read as one journey rather than a sequence of separate landmark arenas.

Its visual progression is:

1. **Local and open** at 28 Ferry Road.
2. **Harbour-focused and scenic** through Days Bay and Williams Park.
3. **Fast and flowing** along Marine Drive.
4. **Gradually more settled** through Rona Bay.
5. **More enclosed and detailed** on the Eastbourne village approach.
6. **Clearly inland and technical** through the final residential turns.
7. **Warm community arrival** at the Eastbourne RSA.

The art must support the gameplay rhythm: long coastal bends first, then a tighter inland finish.

---

## 3. Required visual anchors

### 3.1 28 Ferry Road start

Must communicate:

- A local residential/coastal starting point rather than a racetrack.
- Immediate direction towards Marine Drive.
- Harbour and bush-covered hillside context.

Minimum art:

- Simple start marker or banner.
- Ferry Road street cue.
- Coastal residential edge treatment.
- Strong directional sign towards the main road.
- Warm late-afternoon light.

Do not overbuild the start. Players should begin moving quickly.

### 3.2 Days Bay and Williams Park

This is the first major place-recognition section.

Recognition features:

- Days Bay Wharf visible from the road.
- Williams Park as a green open space beside the route.
- Beach, harbour and bush-covered hills in the correct broad relationship.
- Marine Drive clearly continuing south.

Production approach:

- The wharf should be a standalone or modular landmark outside the drivable road.
- It may extend over water but must never resemble a driveable route.
- Williams Park can be represented through open lawn, mature trees and a simple named sign.
- Final text should be added through code or controlled editing, not trusted to generated imagery.

Gameplay rule:

- Beach and water are visible but unreachable.
- Barriers, walls, vegetation or road geometry must clearly prevent access.

### 3.3 Marine Drive coastal ribbon

This is the dominant visual and driving section.

Recognition features:

- A continuous road hugging the harbour edge.
- Long bends with changing coastline shape.
- Harbour on one side and bush, retaining walls or settlement on the other.
- A strong sense of travelling south rather than circulating around a racetrack.

Art rules:

- Avoid repeated scenery patterns that make the road feel procedural or generic.
- Use changing shoreline curvature, retaining walls, tree groups and small residential cues to create progression.
- Keep road edges exceptionally clear at mobile scale.
- Coastal barriers should look like public-road infrastructure, not full racing-circuit tyre walls.

### 3.4 Rona Bay stretch

Recognition features:

- Rona Bay waterfront character.
- Slightly denser settlement than Days Bay.
- Beach and harbour still visible.
- Clear transition towards Eastbourne village.

This section should feel less open than the main Days Bay run, but not yet fully urban.

Possible visual anchors:

- Named Rona Bay sign.
- Distinct shoreline bend.
- Compact beachfront settlement.
- Low walls, trees and modest homes.

### 3.5 Eastbourne village and shops

This is the main built-up transition before the inland finish.

Recognition features:

- Compact village shopping strip.
- Modest low-rise buildings.
- Clear shopfront rhythm without dense modern advertising.
- More enclosed road character.
- Muritai School area may be used as a supporting local marker where it helps orientation.

Production approach:

- Prefer a modular building set over one giant baked streetscape.
- Use roof shapes, awnings and restrained signs to imply the shops.
- Side roads should be visually closed, simplified or de-emphasised.
- Do not attempt an exact recreation of every shop.

### 3.6 Inland turn near Muritai Road / Oroua Street

This is the key visual change in the final third of the course.

The player must understand immediately that the route is leaving the waterfront.

Recognition and guidance features:

- One dominant inland turn.
- Strong advance directional arrow.
- Coastal view dropping away behind the player.
- Residential street character replacing the open waterfront.
- Buildings, fences and vegetation closing false routes.

The turn should be visually memorable because it changes both the course direction and driving rhythm.

### 3.7 Eastbourne RSA

This is the finish anchor.

Recognition features:

- Community-building character.
- Clearly readable destination.
- A sign or finish treatment identifying the RSA.
- Enough surrounding space for the finish line and results moment.
- Relationship to the final Tuatoru Street approach.

Important text:

- Generate the building and sign structure separately from the final words.
- Add `EASTBOURNE RSA` through code or controlled editing.

Finish tone:

- Small-scale celebration.
- Bunting, a finish board or warm UI flourish is appropriate.
- A crowd is not required.

Finish message:

> **Phew! Just in time for a beer.**

## 4. Environmental language

### Harbour

- Calm blue water.
- Warm late-afternoon reflections.
- Low visual contrast so it does not compete with the road.
- Shoreline must remain visually distinct.

### Hills

- Bush-covered hills rise inland.
- Broad layered green masses.
- Avoid tropical jungle shapes.
- Use enough height and density to give the route its enclosed coastal character.

### Vegetation

Preferred:

- Pōhutukawa and mature coastal trees.
- Native-looking rounded canopy variants.
- Williams Park lawn and established trees.
- Small planted verges.

Avoid:

- Palm-tree resort imagery.
- Uniform suburban ornamental trees.
- Dense vegetation that hides road edges.

### Roadside structures

Use a restrained set of:

- Coastal barriers.
- Retaining walls.
- Simple fences.
- Period-inspired direction signs.
- Modest homes or sheds as background silhouettes.
- Village shopfront modules.
- Residential street edges for the inland finish.

---

## 5. Lighting and palette

Lighting:

- Warm late-afternoon sun.
- Soft long shadows directed consistently.
- Golden highlights on roofs, road edges and Beryl’s chrome.
- No sunset darkness; visibility must remain excellent.

Palette emphasis:

- Harbour blue.
- Warm cream.
- Deep coastal green.
- Faded red accents.
- Sun-warmed asphalt grey.
- Beryl turquoise as the strongest moving colour.

The inland finish may be slightly warmer and more enclosed than the open waterfront, but it must remain part of the same lighting setup.

---

## 6. Road readability

- The route must read as one continuous road from Ferry Road to the RSA.
- Marine Drive should always appear to continue forward.
- Harbour barriers create a strong coastal edge.
- Inland buildings, walls, hills or vegetation create the opposite edge.
- Side streets must not appear equally valid.
- Large advance arrows identify the inland turn and final sharp corners.
- The RSA approach must not be confused with a car park or residential dead end.
- Decorative beach access paths must not look driveable.

No dead end should be visually plausible.

## 7. Initial asset inventory

### Priority 1 — route-defining

- Coastal asphalt treatment.
- Harbour-edge barrier set.
- Inland retaining-wall and verge set.
- Residential fence/building edge set.
- Large directional arrow sign.
- Days Bay Wharf landmark.
- Williams Park sign/open-space treatment.
- Eastbourne shops modular set.
- Eastbourne RSA landmark and controlled sign.

### Priority 2 — place character

- Pōhutukawa variant.
- Native coastal tree variants.
- Beach-edge treatment.
- Harbour-water treatment.
- Coastal house/background variants.
- Days Bay roadside sign.
- Rona Bay roadside sign.
- Eastbourne roadside sign.
- Muritai School-area supporting landmark or sign, if used.
- Residential street props for the inland finish.

### Priority 3 — later decoration

- Static ferry.
- Parked period cars.
- Picnic tables.
- Beachgoers or swimmers.
- People outside shops.
- Additional residential detail.

Priority 3 is out of scope until the course is readable and performant.

## 8. Reference pack required

Before producing major landmarks, gather references for:

- 28 Ferry Road and the turn towards Marine Drive.
- Days Bay Wharf from the road and from above/side.
- Williams Park’s relationship to the road and waterfront.
- Marine Drive coastal barriers, retaining walls and road character.
- Rona Bay shoreline and settlement character.
- Eastbourne shopping area roofline and street relationship.
- Muritai Road / Oroua Street inland-turn character.
- Eastbourne RSA facade, sign and Tuatoru Street approach.

The supplied route screenshot should be retained as the broad route-shape reference.

For each landmark, record which two or three features the user’s father and children are expected to recognise.

---

## 9. Do not include in the first art pass

- Moving ferry.
- Moving traffic.
- Pedestrians.
- Beach access for Beryl.
- Dense modern signs.
- Exact recreation of every shop or house.
- Photoreal water.
- Crowded finish event.
- Racing-circuit tyre walls along the entire route.
- Separate arena-like scenes for each landmark.

This is a public coastal road transformed into a time trial, not a purpose-built circuit.

---

## 10. Art acceptance criteria

- [ ] The course clearly starts at a local Ferry Road setting and joins Marine Drive.
- [ ] Days Bay Wharf and Williams Park are immediately identifiable.
- [ ] Marine Drive reads as one continuous coastal ribbon.
- [ ] Rona Bay feels like a distinct but connected waterfront section.
- [ ] Harbour, road and bush-covered hills have the correct broad relationship.
- [ ] The village approach feels tighter and more enclosed than the waterfront.
- [ ] The inland turn is obvious before the player reaches it.
- [ ] The final streets visually lead towards the RSA rather than a dead end.
- [ ] Eastbourne RSA is a clear finish destination.
- [ ] Beaches and water are visible but not apparently driveable.
- [ ] Lighting reads as warm late afternoon.
- [ ] The route remains obvious on a landscape phone.
- [ ] Beryl remains the strongest focal point.
- [ ] The art feels like a remembered New Zealand summer, not a generic seaside town.
