# Navigation and finish-screen polish — 10 November 2026

Baseline: `be0863d` (PR #79). Ben requested another polish cycle. This pass
targets navigation feedback and the transition from driving to results, with no
new permanent controls, courses, handling changes or rival-speed changes.

## Findings and changes

| Reproduced behaviour | Result |
| --- | --- |
| A car clearly beyond its next checkpoint can receive no explanation; longer routes delay warnings because the threshold is a fixed fraction of the whole course. | All courses show an explicit missed-checkpoint message and contextual reset once the car is clearly beyond the capture zone. The margin uses the capture radius and car size. |
| Manfeild offers reset when gate zero is captured just before progress wraps to a new lap, but fails to offer it after a missed lap finish. | The captured start/finish approach belongs to the new lap; missing the finish beyond its capture zone gets a specific recovery prompt. Expected checkpoints and lap accounting stay unchanged. |
| A low-speed collision primarily encourages a penalised reset. | Contextual help explains holding the existing brake to reverse. The mobile brake caption changes to REVERSE only while actually backing up. Reset remains available. |
| Driving controls remain visible on sprint results, held pedals remain pressed, and canvas result cards allow horn taps through them. | Finishing releases and disables touch driving controls and hides their layer. Finished runs ignore the keyboard horn. Retry creates neutral, enabled controls again. |
| Remutaka and Ōtaki omit recovery penalties from their result explanation. | Their result summaries include the recovery seconds, with spacing checked on a small landscape phone. |
| Typing `sarah watts` into the Eastbourne name field produces `rtt`; Phaser captures driving letters and Space before the browser can enter them. | Key events stay inside the editable field. Normal typing saves `SARAH WATTS`, including the space; keyboard driving works again after retry. |

## Evidence and verification

The navigation regressions use explicit game-state fixtures to reproduce missed
gates and lap-boundary cases. They establish the feedback and recovery contracts,
not that an uncoached player finds every route intuitively. Finish fixtures use
actual touch events, a held throttle, result buttons and ordinary keyboard typing.

`playtest/navigation-finish.mjs --baseline` captured the original navigation and
results behaviour. The final regression checks all four missed-checkpoint cases,
both Manfeild lap-boundary cases, all three sprint result screens, penalty copy,
held-input release, ignored horn taps, name entry/persistence, retry and restored
keyboard input. It also holds the real touch brake and verifies reverse motion
and its caption. This regression is included in the Quality workflow.

Inspected the small-phone screenshots: missed-checkpoint guidance uses two
deliberate lines between the HUD and utility controls; result screens lose the
large driving buttons; the recovery explanation fits above retry/change course.

Quick quality checks, safe-recovery/storage/camera contracts, Eastbourne geometry
and recovery checks, responsive result cards and the production build pass.
The full polish browser runner still completes all four reference drives at the
same measured times: Eastbourne 69.650 s, Manfeild 52.767 s, Remutaka 151.333 s,
Ōtaki 203.117 s. Slower-retry split retention, contextual mobile recovery, the
second Ōtaki seed and the no-traffic diagnostic also pass with no browser errors.
CI additionally runs the complete touch-only finish/name/retry journey; its name
step now types characters rather than bypassing keyboard handling with a fill.

Real-phone ergonomics, unaided navigation and subjective pacing still need human
review. The next useful polish evidence would be an uncoached Ōtaki town/finish
approach and a Manfeild run after the early overtakes. These fixtures do not
justify changing scenery or race balance on their own.
